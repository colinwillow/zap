# THE ZELDA SKELETON -- how a whole Zelda is built, and a skeleton for Korajin (research, Oct 28 2026)

> *"Thinking of things in temples is probably good -- you go somewhere underground, somewhere on earth, somewhere in the sky... it made me think of
> Zelda. We're kind of building a Zelda-style game, which is fine, I love Zelda. How can we pick apart a classical Zelda: how many temples, within a
> temple how many levels, the progression of the whole game, chapters, missions, sub-missions, side missions, powers, evolutions, advancement --
> so we can create a skeleton: how many chapters we want, how many sub-chapters."*

This is a STARTING SKELETON for him to cut, not a decision. The numbers in Part 1 are the games' own; Part 3 is a proposal.

---

## PART 1 -- WHAT THE CLASSIC ZELDAS ACTUALLY CONTAIN

| game | main dungeons | how they split | quest tokens | extras |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A Link to the Past** (1991) | 3 + 7 + finale | Light World: 3 dungeons for the PENDANTS, then the Master Sword; Dark World: 7 dungeons for the CRYSTALS, then Ganon's Tower | 3 pendants, 7 crystals | 24 heart pieces; 20 hearts max |
| **Link's Awakening** (1993) | 8 (+ the Color Dungeon in DX) | one long chain, each dungeon opened by the last one's item | 8 Instruments of the Sirens (wake the Wind Fish) | secret seashells (20 earn a sword); heart pieces; trading sequence |
| **Ocarina of Time** (1998) | 3 + 5 + finale, plus 3 mini-dungeons | CHILD: 3 dungeons for the Spiritual Stones; ADULT (7 years later): 5 temples for the Medallions; then Ganon's Castle. Mini: Bottom of the Well, Ice Cavern, Gerudo Training Ground (no boss) | 3 stones, 6 medallions | 36 heart pieces (= 9 hearts); 100 Gold Skulltulas (44 in dungeons, 56 outside), traded for rewards at 10/20/30/40/50/100 |
| **The Minish Cap** (2004) | ~6 (5 + the final castle) | a short, dense chain | 4 Elements (sword upgrades) | Kinstones (fuse with townspeople -- the side-quest engine); heart pieces |
| **Breath of the Wild** (2017) | 4 Divine Beasts + the castle | OPEN: any order after the tutorial plateau | 4 beasts | 120 shrines (mini-dungeons, ~5-15 min each), 900 Korok seeds |
| **Tears of the Kingdom** (2023) | 5 temples + the depths | open, three LAYERS of map: SKY, SURFACE, DEPTHS | the sages | 152 shrines, ~1000 Korok seeds, lightroots |

**What every one of them shares:**
1. **A COUNT of big dungeons between 4 and 10**, almost always split into TWO OR THREE ACTS (LttP 3 + 7, OoT 3 + 5, BotW plateau + 4 + castle).
   The act break is a WORLD CHANGE: the Dark World, seven years later, the sky opening. That break is the game's midpoint twist.
2. **Each dungeon pays a QUEST TOKEN** (pendant, instrument, medallion) that counts toward the final door. The count IS the progress bar.
3. **Each dungeon pays an ITEM** (hookshot, bow, iron boots) that is both the key to THAT dungeon and a key to new places in the overworld. The
   item is what makes the world open up -- the "Metroid" half of Zelda.
4. **Each boss pays a HEART CONTAINER**, and the overworld hides HEART PIECES (24-36 of them, 4 per heart).
5. **One BIG COLLECTIBLE SET** with rewards at milestones (Skulltulas, seashells, Koroks) -- the thing completionists chase.
6. **Small mini-dungeons** between the big ones: caves, wells, shrines -- 5-15 minutes, one idea each, no big boss.
7. **A town and its people** whose side quests pay upgrades (wallets, bottles, masks, Kinstones).
8. **A trading sequence** or a long side thread that runs the whole game.

## PART 2 -- THE ANATOMY OF ONE TEMPLE

From Mark Brown's *Boss Keys* series (he mapped every Zelda dungeon as a graph of keys and locks) and the designers' own talks:

**The recipe (nearly every dungeon since Link's Awakening):**
1. **ENTRANCE / HUB** -- a central room the dungeon keeps returning you to (Twilight Princess is built almost entirely on hubs; the return is what
   makes a linear route FEEL non-linear).
2. **FIRST HALF** -- rooms solvable with what you walked in with. Small keys open small doors, **usually one locked door available at a time** (Ocarina
   of Time), so the player never chooses wrong and never gets stuck.
3. **THE MINI-BOSS** guards the **DUNGEON ITEM**, roughly half way.
4. **SECOND HALF** -- the item is TAUGHT (a safe room where it is the only answer), then TESTED (with enemies), then TWISTED (used in a way you didn't
   expect). This is *kishōtenketsu*: introduce, develop, twist, conclude.
5. **LOOP-BACK** -- the item opens a shortcut back to the hub. No walking back through cleared rooms.
6. **THE BOSS KEY** (the "Nightmare Key" since Link's Awakening: it opens only the boss door).
7. **THE BOSS** is beaten WITH the dungeon item -- the final exam of the new verb.
8. **REWARD:** heart container + quest token, then a warp out.

**The numbers (typical 3D Zelda temple):**

| | typical | notes |
|---|---|---|
| floors / levels | 2-4 | vertical: basement, ground, upper, roof -- fits the planes rule |
| rooms | 12-25 | Ocarina's temples sit around 15-25 |
| small keys | 3-6 | Brown counts the original Zelda as too many; Zelda II had the right count |
| mini-bosses | 1 (sometimes 2) | guards the item |
| item | 1 | the temple's new verb |
| boss | 1 | uses the item |
| collectibles inside | a map, a compass, 3-5 hidden tokens (OoT: 3-5 Skulltulas per dungeon) | rewards for exploring |
| play time | 30-60 min first time | a mini-dungeon / shrine is 5-15 |

## PART 3 -- A SKELETON FOR KORAJIN (proposed -- his to cut)

**Target size: Link's Awakening / Minish Cap scale -- 8 temples, about 10-12 hours.** Attainable, and it is the scale where every temple can be
hand-made and the whole-piece rule can still hold. BotW-scale (120 shrines) is out of reach and not the game.

**It maps straight onto what Korajin already has:** his three PLANES (underground, earth, sky) become the act structure, the Jinra beacons are the
quest tokens, the restoration is the reward you see, the statues and the chalice are each temple's last beat, and the ship and the dragon are the
"items" that open the world.

### The acts (= chapters)

| act | plane | regions | what changes at the end |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PROLOGUE** | the fallen ground | the Red Hollows (home) | 1 tutorial temple. Grumbo wakes his first statue; the uncle, the one floating building |
| **ACT I -- THE GROUND** | earth | 2 regions x 2 temples | the beacons light: **THE SHIP FLIES** (the world opens to the sea and the air) |
| **ACT II -- THE BELOW** | underground | 2 regions x 1 temple + the deep temple | the old Aztec / Maya-style stone gods under the world; **the dragon is tamed** |
| **ACT III -- THE SKY** | the floating world | 2 regions x 1 temple | the restored sky world rises; the last beacons align |
| **FINALE** | the Kūkyo citadel | 1 | the central tower wakes |

**Temple count: 1 + 4 + 3 + 2 + 1 = 11 places with bosses**, of which **8 are "great temples"** that pay a token (prologue and finale are
special). That is the Link's Awakening count. Each act ends on a world-changing event, like LttP's Dark World and OoT's seven years.

### Per REGION (the sub-chapter) -- the repeatable unit

| piece | count per region | what it is in Korajin |
|---|---|---|
| a fallen village to restore | 1 | the town: shops, people, the restoration cinematic at the end |
| townspeople to awaken (artifacts) | 4-6 | the side-quest engine (Minish Cap's Kinstones): bring an artifact, a merchant becomes a Jin |
| the enemy's TORCH TOWER | 1 | the region's boss climb; light it = the beacon |
| a GREAT TEMPLE | 1-2 | Part 2's anatomy; pays the item + heart container + Jinra seal |
| mini-dungeons (caves, wells, shrines) | 2-3 | one idea each, 5-15 min; pay heart pieces / Jinra |
| side missions | 3-5 | people's errands, races, a fishing hole, a trader's chain |
| collectible set | ~12 | (Part 4) |
| heart pieces | 3-4 | |

### Per TEMPLE -- the build spec (one temple = one sandbox with a DONE WHEN, per the whole-piece rule)

- **3 floors** (below, level, above) and **15-20 rooms** in Part 2's order: hub, first half, mini-boss + ITEM, taught / tested / twisted, loop-back,
  boss key, boss with the item.
- **3-5 small keys** (one locked door open at a time), **1 boss key**, **3 hidden collectibles**.
- **The statue beat (STATUES.md):** stone guardians stand in the temple from the start. Taking the temple's relic (the chalice) wakes them for the
  escape or the gauntlet before the boss.
- **The boss** is a Kūkyo construct (an Utsuro): stone god / temple guardian scale, beaten with the temple's item.
- **The theme** is its new verb (LEVELDESIGN.md layer 4).

### Progression (powers, evolutions, advancement)

| track | how many | where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| **temple ITEMS** (new verbs) | 8 | one per great temple -- e.g. grapple / zip line, wall run boots, glider, the bow, the spirit lantern, the rocket |
| **SWORD tiers** | 3 | the katana forged up at act breaks (Minish Cap's Picori Blade -> White Sword -> Four Sword) |
| **JIN POWERS** (bought with Jinra) | 6-8, 3 levels each | the charge strikes, the tornado, the zoom, the deflect, the green fire |
| **hearts** | 3 at start -> 20 max | 8-10 boss containers + ~28-32 heart pieces (OoT has 36) |
| **mounts / vehicles** | 3 | the ship (act I), the dragon (act II), the board |
| **money** | doubloons | wallets as side-quest rewards |

### Collectibles and side content (the completionist layer)

- **One big set:** say **100 Jinra wisps** (OoT's 100 Gold Skulltulas) traded at a shrine at 10 / 20 / 30 / 50 / 100 for upgrades.
- **Heart pieces:** ~32.
- **Townspeople awakened:** ~24 (6 regions x 4).
- **A trading thread** that runs across the whole game (Link's Awakening's trade sequence), ending in a sword or a dragon saddle.

### Scale check (what this means to build)

| | count |
|---|---|
| acts | 3 + prologue + finale |
| regions | 6 + home + citadel |
| great temples | 8 (+ the tutorial and the citadel) |
| mini-dungeons | ~15 |
| bosses | ~11 temple bosses + ~6 torch towers + mini-bosses |
| enemy kinds (each with its stone twin) | ~12-15 |
| side missions | ~25 |

**The first thing to build is ONE great temple, end to end, as its own sandbox** (whole-piece rule): 3 floors, the hub, a key loop, a mini-boss,
an item that is taught, tested and twisted, the statue beat, and a boss that needs the item. If one temple is fun on its own, the skeleton is
just more of it.

### Open (his calls)
- 8 great temples, or fewer and bigger (6 like Minish Cap)?
- Linear like Link's Awakening, or open like BotW within each act (any order among an act's regions)? The skeleton above works either way;
  an act-gated open order is the middle ground.
- Do the three planes stay as the acts (ground, below, sky), or does each REGION have all three layers (TotK's sky / surface / depths)?
- What the 8 temple items are.
