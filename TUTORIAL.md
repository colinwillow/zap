# THE TUTORIAL -- how Zelda teaches, and what that means for Korajin (his question, Oct 29 2026)

> *"A tutorial level where you learn to play one step at a time -- basic locomotion, flips, tricks, melee, wall runs, long and short jumps, how to
> evade, counter, charge your attacks. How does Zelda do it, since you don't have all your abilities at the beginning? Are tutorials progressive
> throughout the game?"*

## Yes: Zelda teaches in two places
1. **A contained opening area teaches the CORE, and nothing else.** Breath of the Wild's Great Plateau: a walled region you cannot leave until you
   have climbed, glided, fought, cooked and used the four runes -- each in a shrine built around one of them. Ocarina of Time's Kokiri Forest:
   jump, climb, roll, the sword, the shield, Z-targeting, then the Deku Tree as the first exam. Wind Waker's Outset Island and the forest haven
   do the same. No separate "tutorial mode": the opening IS the tutorial, and it has a story reason to exist.
2. **Every later ability is taught WHERE IT IS FOUND.** The item in a dungeon is introduced in a safe room (a gap the hookshot crosses with nothing
   at stake), developed (it gets harder), twisted (a new use), and examined by the boss (who cannot be beaten without it). That is
   ZELDA_STRUCTURE.md part 2, and it is a tutorial spread across the whole game. Later abilities never get a screen of text: the room is the lesson.
3. **How it teaches:** show before tell (an enemy uses the move on you first, or you see the ledge before you can reach it); safe first, then
   stakes; a short prompt only the first time and only for the gesture (never the rule); and the level shape forces the move (you cannot pass
   until you have done it once).

## So for Korajin
- **Level 0 -- "the dojo-in-ruins" or the village before the fall** (our Great Plateau): contained, short, a story reason (the boy training the day
  the sorceress comes). One room or yard per core skill, in this order, each with one prompt the first time:
  1. move, look, the camera
  2. jump, double jump; a short jump and a long (sprint) jump over gaps sized from the move table
  3. flips (the left flicks), the roll / dodge
  4. the sword: the flick strikes, the combo, the guard and the PARRY (a training post that swings back)
  5. the charge and its releases (lunge, whirl, leap)
  6. evade and counter (a sparring partner who telegraphs)
  7. the first wall jump (a chimney) -- the wall RUN comes later, where it is found
  8. the exam: a short course that needs all of it, and a first real fight
- **Everything else is found in the world and taught there:** the wall run, the bow (the fletcher's range), the zoom, the tornado, the rope
  bridge, the dragon. Each gets an introduce -> develop -> twist -> exam room in the level where you get it (LEVELDESIGN.md's teach / develop /
  twist / conclude).
- **Prompts**: the gesture drawn on the pad itself (the pads already have rings and labels), once, then gone. Never a pop-up of rules.
